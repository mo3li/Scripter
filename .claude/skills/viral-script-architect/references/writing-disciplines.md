# Writing Disciplines Toolkit

Source: the user's summary of «تخصصات الكتابة» by سالم بن عقيل. This file keeps only what applies to video scripts. History, careers, book lists and game writing are left out.

Core idea: **writing is organized thinking.** If you can organize the idea, you can write it.

Load this when:
- a script needs a specific craft: marketing/sales, comedy, drama or story, news, or an emotional/creative piece
- the user wants more ideas from one product or topic

## 1. Content writing (educate, explain, solve)

- **If you can't explain it simply, you didn't understand it.** Understand the idea first, then write.
- **Know the brief before writing:** goal, tone, exact details, what to avoid. If it's missing, fill the gaps with sensible defaults and state them (§22 of SKILL.md), or ask one question when the gap changes the whole script.
- **Skills:** research from reliable sources, know the audience, simplify with concrete examples and no filler, logical order, and natural keywords.
- **What makes an idea stick** ("Made to Stick" style): simple, surprising, credible, emotional, told as a story, clear. Success = understood, remembered, easy to share.
- **The 5 angles for any topic** (one topic becomes 5 videos):

| Angle | Question | Example (Dubai off-plan) |
|---|---|---|
| Social | How did it change people's behaviour? | ليه الناس بقت تشتري على الخريطة بدل الجاهز؟ |
| Story | What happened to someone who used it? | اللي حصل مع واحد استلم شقته قبل الشهادة |
| Informative | 5 facts about it | ٥ حاجات لازم تعرفها عن حساب الضمان |
| Emotional | How did it change my life? | *(real story only)* |
| Technical | The secret behind how it works | إزاي فلوسك بتتحمى قانونيًا لحد الاستلام |

Combine with the 7 angles in `idea-multiplication.md`.

## 2. Marketing / sales writing

- **Sell the feeling the product creates, not the product.**
- **90% of the work is deleting.** The first sentence rarely survives. Cut every word that doesn't serve the goal.
- **Performance over pretty language.** Write to drive action, not to impress.
- **The headline, which is the hook in video, decides everything.** If it fails, the rest isn't seen.
- **No worn-out phrases.** Replace "جودة عالية" with a concrete picture: "هتعيش معاك سنين".
- **Length by medium:**

| Medium | Length |
|---|---|
| Social post | ~3–12 words |
| Paid ad | ~30-character headline + ~90-character description |
| Video ad | 60s or less ≈ 120 words |
| Instagram caption | ~50 words |
| Email | ~150 words |
| Landing page | ~300 words or more: hook line, product, proof, features, CTA |
| Billboard / on-screen line | 5–8 words |

- **The 6 buying triggers.** Use them only when they're true. Never invent scarcity or deadlines.
  - **Scarcity**: limited quantity.
  - **Urgency**: the offer ends at a real date.
  - **Discount**: a double-edged sword, because a permanent discount cheapens the product.
  - **Price comparison**: old price next to the new one.
  - **Proof**: real users or known names.
  - **Bundle**: several items for less than buying them separately.
- **The CTA matches the product:**
  - simple product → "اطلبه دلوقتي"
  - subscription → "اشترك دلوقتي" (the enemy is procrastination)
  - premium service → "انضم لنا" / "تعالى جرّب"
  - real estate or land → "سجّل اهتمامك" (they need details before deciding)
- **Maslow level of the audience:**
  - basic needs → talk about need and practicality
  - middle → belonging to a group
  - top → uniqueness and standing out
- **Value equation:** value = (dream result × likelihood of success) ÷ (time × effort or cost). In a sales script, raise the result and the proof, and shrink the time, effort and cost.
- **Aristotle's persuasion triad:**
  - **Logic**: evidence, numbers, demos.
  - **Emotion**: a story or a mental picture.
  - **Credibility**: reputation, a trusted person, social proof.
  - Strong sales scripts touch all three.
- **Language by economic segment:**
  - high-end → refined, exclusive, sells distinction
  - middle → light and entertaining
  - budget → direct and practical, shows the cost is low
- **Writing voices to borrow:**
  - **Factual**: a clear promise and how it works.
  - **Smart and refined**: one precise detail that says everything. Classic example: the Rolls-Royce ad where the loudest noise at 60 mph is the electric clock.
  - **Story and emotion**: "They laughed when I sat down at the piano, but when I started to play…"
- **Give value, give value, then ask** ("Jab, Jab, Jab, Right Hook"): most content gives, and the ask comes after trust.

## 3. Creative writing (make them feel)

- **"No tears in the writer, no tears in the reader. No surprise for the writer, no surprise for the reader."** If the line doesn't move you, it won't move them.
- **The 4 creative aims.** Pick one per script:
  - **رثاء**: grief over a loss.
  - **شجب**: anger at something wrong, exposing it.
  - **ترغيب**: making something beautiful or desirable.
  - **إضحاك**: making them laugh, or criticizing indirectly.
- **3 modes:** free narration (spontaneous, like thinking out loud, which is yapping), specialized narration (script or article format), and poetic (dense, rhythmic, fewest words).
- **Originality matters most here.** AI helps with content and marketing, but creative and personal pieces need the creator's real experience.

## 4. Journalistic writing (news-style and explainer videos)

- **Facts, not opinion:**
  - "الرحلة 512 اتأخرت ٩٠ دقيقة" is news. "شركة الطيران كعادتها بتتأخر" is opinion.
  - In news-style scripts, lead with the numbers and facts.
- **Headline test:** brief, clear, important. "انقطاع المياه في الرياض" beats "تأثر خدمات مرافق حيوية".
- **What makes a topic stand out:**
  - **Oddity**: "man bites dog", not "dog bites man".
  - **Trend**: tie it to what people are talking about.
  - **Innovation**: a local fix for a known problem.
- **Inverted pyramid:** the most important fact first (who, what, when, where), then supporting details (quotes, numbers), then background. Viewers get the point even if they leave early.
- **Warning** (the 1938 "War of the Worlds" panic): a convincing voice without a clear label makes people believe fiction. Always make it clear when a script is a dramatization, a hypothetical or satire.

## 5. Comedy writing

- **Laughter comes from a sudden, fast contradiction.** Types: exaggeration, wordplay, sarcasm, embarrassment.
- **A joke has 3 units:**
  1. **Idea**: the observation.
  2. **Misdirection**: lead the viewer to a logical expectation.
  3. **Punchline**: break the expectation with a surprising, clear meaning.

  Examples:
  - A guy in a fancy restaurant studies the menu for half an hour (idea + misdirection), then orders just a Pepsi (punch).
  - "لازم تلتزم في التمرين!" "طب جدولك إيه؟" "من الساونا للجاكوزي وأروّح." (idea → misdirection → punch)
- **Stack punchlines:** after one punch, add a second shorter one on the same setup, each bigger than the last, with no re-setup.
- **Timing lives in the script:** use punctuation, line breaks and pauses ("…") to mark when to stop, slow down or speed up.
- **The creator's persona is part of the joke:**
  - If people only laugh *with* you, they won't feel your presence.
  - If they only laugh *at* you, they won't respect you.
  - Mix both, and self-deprecation first.
- **Know the audience:** jokes play on their fears and interests. What makes one group laugh may offend another.
- **Output for a comedy request:** 2–3 options (idea / misdirection / punch / second punch), pick the strongest, say in one line why it works, then give the short-video version in a code block.

## 6. Drama / scene writing (skits, story reels)

- **Drama imitates actions, not people.** Characters are revealed by what they do and choose.
- **Show, don't tell:**
  - A character never states their feelings; the situation reveals them.
  - Let dialogue be about one thing on the surface (papers, the house, dinner) while the real conflict (absence, betrayal) sits underneath.
- **Every element must be used.** A shown object, line or character that never pays off feels like waste and loses attention.
- **Mini 3-act structure:** world/problem → conflict/escalation → resolution/new state.
- **Each scene:** the character's goal, an obstacle, and a turn at the end.
- **Escalate with new information,** and let events follow from the characters' decisions: no coincidences, no easy fixes, no explanatory dialogue.

## 7. Literary depth (for emotional or personal-brand pieces)

- **3 layers:**
  1. **Issue**: the human question (how do people deal with loss?).
  2. **Embodiment**: a character, a situation, a conflict.
  3. **Meaning**: what the viewer takes away.
- **Write the feeling without naming it.** Avoid "حزين، اشتاق، اتألم". Show a concrete detail instead, like a shopping list in the mother's handwriting ending with "متنسيش العيش", folded into a bag without a word.
- **Point of view:** you, an all-knowing narrator, the hero, the opponent, even an object. A fresh narrator can make an old idea feel new.

## Applying it to short scripts (checklist)

- Hook = headline: brief, clear, important. Add oddity, trend or innovation when true.
- 5 angles → 5 videos from one topic.
- Delete until every line has a job.
- Sell the feeling. Use a concrete picture, not an adjective.
- Comedy: idea → misdirection → punch → bigger punch.
- Drama: show, don't tell. Everything shown gets used.
- Structure: mini 3 acts. Put the key fact first in news or educational pieces.
- Persuasion: logic + emotion + credibility.
- CTA fits the product type. Triggers only when true.
- Language fits the segment and the Maslow level.
- Sales: raise result and proof, shrink time, effort and cost.
- AI assists and the creator's real voice and experience lead.
