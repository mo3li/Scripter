# Hook Library

Use these as mechanisms, not templates to paste. Pick the mechanism that fits the idea, then write it in the creator's voice. A hook is only good if the video pays it off.

## What a strong hook does (in 1–3 seconds)

1. **Selects the viewer**: they instantly know "this is for me".
2. **Opens a gap**: something they want to know and don't yet.
3. **Signals payoff**: there's a reason to believe the answer is coming, soon.
4. **Is specific**: a number, a name, a concrete situation beats abstraction.

Quick test: if you remove the rest of the video, does the hook alone make someone *need* the next sentence? If it could open 1,000 other videos, it's too generic.

## Hook stacking (short-form)

The strongest openings align three layers, each adding something:

- **Spoken line**: the curiosity gap.
- **On-screen text**: the viewer selector or the stakes (not a word-for-word copy of the spoken line).
- **First visual**: movement, a prop, a result, a face reaction, a screen recording.

Example:
- Spoken: "الغلطة دي بتخلّي ٨٠٪ من الناس يقفلوا الفيديو بتاعك." *(only with a real source; otherwise drop the number)*
- Text: "لو الـ Retention بتاعك وحش"
- Visual: close-up on a retention graph dropping.

## Clichés to avoid (unless used ironically or they genuinely fit)

- "Wait for it…" / "استنى للآخر"
- "You won't believe…" / "مش هتصدق"
- "Stop scrolling!" / "وقّف سكرول"
- "Let me tell you a secret" / "هقولك سر" (as a standalone line with no specific)
- "3 tips to…" with nothing specific about the tips

## Delivering hook options

When giving multiple hooks, vary the **mechanism**, not just the wording. Label each with its mechanism so the user can choose by strategy, e.g.:

1. (Contrarian) …
2. (Story) …
3. (Proof-first) …
